## Users

Primary: owners of enthusiast and collector vehicles in South Florida who are considering selling. The document that defined this business is specific about who is worth serving — not the person who *needs* to sell, but the person who does not want to run the sale themselves:

- Busy professionals. Physicians, attorneys, executives, founders, retired enthusiasts. Roughly $300k/yr and up.
- They own something in the $40k–$250k+ band: Porsche (911, Cayman, Boxster, GT cars, air-cooled), BMW M, Mercedes-AMG, Audi RS, Corvette, Shelby, Supra, Lotus, Ferrari, Lamborghini, McLaren, Singer, Japanese classics, special editions.
- They value convenience, confidence and trust over squeezing the last dollar, and would rather pay a fee than spend twenty hours answering Marketplace messages.

Their situation on arrival: they have either been offered a disappointing trade number, or they are staring down the work of a private sale. The job they are doing on the page is deciding whether to trust a stranger with a car worth more than most people's savings.

The trust gap is the central design problem. The operator is new and unknown; the visitor is being asked to hand over a $120,000 car.

## Product Purpose

A white-glove sales service for enthusiast vehicles. The operator handles valuation, marketplace selection, physical preparation, photography and video, documentation, the listing write-up, comment and bidder management, negotiation, and transaction and shipping coordination — so the owner realises more of what the car is worth without doing the work.

Success for the client: a materially better outcome than the trade offer, with almost no hours spent.
Success for the business: repeat referrals from specialty shops and satisfied owners.

## Positioning

**Equity recovery, not photography.** The client is not paying for pictures; they are paying to extract value that already exists in an asset they own but do not know how to sell well. The register is closer to a financial advisor than to a marketing agency.

The differentiator is combination. A detailer cleans. A photographer shoots. An auction site hosts. A dealer buys. Very few operators do valuation, preparation, media, documentation, venue selection, listing and negotiation as one engagement.

**Marketplace selection is part of the product, not an assumption.** Not every enthusiast car belongs on Bring a Trailer. Documented, original, rare and collector cars suit BaT; newer, modified and modern performance cars suit Cars & Bids; higher-mileage, niche and local cars often do better in a private sale; some cars genuinely do better on dealer consignment; Ferrari, McLaren, Lamborghini and Porsche GT cars may belong with an exotic dealer. Choosing the venue is a service, and a business that only lists on BaT is not performing it.

## Operating Context

- The visitor is vetting a stranger, not comparing vendors. Credibility is the whole job of the surface.
- Acquisition is expected to run through referral partners rather than paid search: independent Porsche/BMW/Ferrari specialists, performance shops, detailers, ceramic coating and PPF installers, wrap and wheel shops, body shops, and luxury dealers whose declined trade offers create the exact moment this service is for. Also Cars & Coffee, PCA and BMW CCA chapters, and owners with visibly poor existing listings.
- The service is staged so the client is not abandoned if the first venue fails: preparation, then auction listing, then private-party listing, then dealer network placement.
- Region: South Florida.

## Capabilities and Constraints

Confirmed:
- Free 30-minute consultation to determine whether the car is worth taking on, and which venue suits it.
- **Prep fee: $750, flat, regardless of vehicle.** Covers hard costs.
- **Success fee: a flat amount set by the vehicle's value band**, owed only on sale. There is no percentage and no share of the sale price — the operator removed the 10%-of-gain component on 2026-08-10. Bands: under $40,000 quoted; $40–70k $750; $70–120k $1,250; $120–250k $2,000; above $250,000 quoted.
- The listing standard: 100+ photographs, cold-start and driving video, undercarriage, walkaround, maintenance timeline, Carfax review, ownership history, disclosed known flaws, professional write-up. Paint-meter readings, compression/leakdown and paint-correction reporting were removed from the published standard on 2026-08-10.
- **Verification is a precondition, not a formality.** Government photo ID and current registration must be seen and must match before work begins; if they are not provided the engagement does not start and the prep fee is not refunded. Where the seller is not the registered owner, the owner attends in person or joins a video call showing ID.
- **No checks.** Payment is confirmed and cleared before any in-person meeting.
- Secondary services available: pre-purchase inspection coordination, paint correction, ceramic coating, storage, transport, record digitisation, vehicle sourcing, buying consultation, auction representation.
- Launch niche: the full enthusiast list (user's decision), not narrowed to one marque.

## Product Principles

1. **Sell the spread, not the service.** The argument is the gap between a trade offer and a well-run sale. Everything else is how that gap gets closed.
2. **Never promise a number.** The founding document is explicit: promise maximum exposure and professional presentation, never a sale price. A falling market must not be able to turn a promise into a grievance.
3. **Borrow trust before you have your own.** With no track record, credibility comes from the visible rigour of the standard, the transparency of the process, and the willingness to say what the service will not do.
4. **Venue selection is the expertise.** Naming the cases where the answer is *not* an auction is the single most credible thing this business can say.
5. **Align the fee with the outcome.** The pricing structure — small prep fee, success fee tied to a documented baseline — is itself an argument, and should be presented as one.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
